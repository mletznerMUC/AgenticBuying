SDAW against the agentic protocols

A market deep dive inside the out-of-home channel: SDAW (Standardisierter Datensatz zur Außenwerbung), the German poster industry's exchange format, measured field by field against AAMP (Agentic Advertising Management Protocols) and AdCP (Ad Context Protocol). The finding that organizes everything else is that the gap runs in both directions — neither side is a superset of the other.

How this page was assembled, and what that costs it

SDAW's specification is a members-only document of the trade association that issues it, so no primary artifact for it is publicly readable. Every field-level statement on this page derives from one open-source toolchain published between 2011 and 2013 whose XML definitions quote the specification, cross-read across three repositories by the same author — not from the specification itself. reported Roughly 220 of the at least 495 bytes of the master-data record, and about 24 of the roughly 30 file types, are not publicly documented at all, including one unbroken 140-byte block.

Sources: open-sdaw · sdxist · sdaw-import · last verified 2026-09-08

speculative Whether SDAW is still actively exchanged in 2026 is unsettled. No public source discusses the format after roughly 2013, and the association that owns it — the Fachverband Außenwerbung, renamed B|A|M Bundesverband Aussenmedien in 2025 — does not mention SDAW on its current site. Its continued use in German poster trading is asserted by out-of-home glossaries rather than by the association, and the German-language sources that describe it do not date their descriptions.

Sources: B|A|M Bundesverband Aussenmedien · outmaxx out-of-home glossary · schwarz-aw out-of-home glossary · last verified 2026-09-08

The comparison below is therefore asymmetric in evidence, not only in coverage: the AdCP and AAMP columns rest on open repositories that anyone can re-read, and the SDAW column rests on a reconstruction. Read the SDAW side as a working hypothesis until the specification itself is in hand.

Three standards, three eras

SDAW was built for a file exchange between a lessee and an agency; AAMP and AdCP were built for two software agents negotiating. That difference shows up in every row below, and it is the reason a mapping is not a migration.

Two properties of this record cause most of the mapping difficulty, and both are structural rather than cosmetic. First, site identity is scoped to the lessee: the site number is unique only inside a three-character lessee prefix, so there is no market-wide panel identifier to hand an agent. Second, time is an index rather than a date: the booking-period type plus a period number resolves to calendar dates only through the annually re-issued decade plan and the municipality's pasting block, whose start dates and head-and-tail exceptions change from year to year. In the reference implementation those dates are carried as data, not derived from a rule.

Source: sdaw-import · last verified 2026-09-08

The four rows that decide the rest

Panel identity. AdCP's pre-release out-of-home metrics block can name a panel in a delivery report, using an operator face identifier, but no property type or identifier type exists for a static panel in the released schemas. The consequence is precise: a panel can be reported on and cannot be discovered or sold. AAMP inherits a frames targeting unit and an invalid-frame error code from its OpenDirect base without a frame object underneath, so the same gap is there for a different reason.

Inventory geo. Both protocols treat coordinates as something a buyer sends or a report attaches, not as an attribute of the thing being sold. In AdCP the addition of structured geo to properties and placements is a tracked issue rather than a field, which is why a German panel's coordinates currently have to travel in an extension object.

The posting-cycle grid. Decades, pasting blocks and in-and-out-of-charge dates have no representation on either side. AdCP's static out-of-home draft states outright that the protocol will not maintain a posting calendar, which pushes the decade plan into the seller's own systems and means every external interface has to publish concrete start and end dates.

The audience currency. AdCP's out-of-home metrics require the basis of an impression estimate to be declared, and name a handful of national measurement bodies as examples; the German figure, Plakatseher pro Stelle, which replaced the older G-Wert from 1 January 2013, appears in neither protocol. A German seller can carry it as a seller-modeled estimate, which is honest and is not the same thing as a recognized currency.

The distribution is the commercially interesting part. SDAW's weakest published steps — posting order, proof of posting, invoicing — are where AdCP's pre-release work for physical media is strongest, and its strongest published steps — panel master data and cycle-accurate availability — are where both agentic protocols have nothing.

The gap in both directions

What SDAW would need to be agentically transactable

  • A request and response surface. The unit of exchange is a zipped file, and nothing public describes a transport, let alone a task lifecycle.
  • Credentials, signatures and idempotency. Integrity rests on total records; agent-mediated buying assumes signed, replayable, idempotent operations.
  • Digital playout concepts. There is no screen, loop, slot or share of time in the format; the mechanical-changer construction type describes a rotating frame, not a playlist.
  • An impression currency. One static performance value per panel, and billing in days rather than in contacts.
  • A larger identifier space. Ninety-nine panels per site, lessee-scoped site numbers, and no market-wide identity.
  • Robustness against positional drift. A single wrong byte shifts every following field, and the character set is nowhere stated.
  • A public specification. The unknown file types and record bytes cannot be obtained without association access.

Sources: open-sdaw · sdxist · last verified 2026-09-08

What the protocols would need to represent German classic out-of-home

  • A bookable panel object. AdCP can report a panel it cannot discover or sell; AAMP has an inherited frames unit with nothing behind it.
  • A static out-of-home property type in AdCP, without which static inventory has no seller-authorization path of the kind other channels use.
  • Geo on inventory. Coordinates are targeting input and evidence metadata on both sides.
  • A posting-cycle grid. Decades, pasting blocks and in-and-out-of-charge dates have no representation.
  • Illumination and physical dimensions on inventory, rather than illuminated hours in a report and print specifications on a creative.
  • A place for national currencies without a joint industry standard. The German measurement figure is named in neither protocol.
  • Cost per frame in AAMP, which its own OpenDirect base has and its pricing enumeration does not.

Sources: AdCP static out-of-home design draft · IAB Tech Lab Agentic Deals API · OpenDirect 2.1 · last verified 2026-09-08

Where the nearest out-of-home data model actually sits

The closest thing to an IAB out-of-home data model is not AAMP but OpenDirect 2.1, built in 2024 with the out-of-home trade bodies: it has frames, named audience datasources, share of time, cost per frame and a proof-of-posting statistics object. AAMP inherits that vocabulary shell through its OpenDirect-based order surface without binding out-of-home semantics to it. AdCP's static out-of-home draft reaches the same conclusion from the other side, stating that it borrows OpenDirect's vocabulary — lead times, flight bounds, frame references and proof of posting as a first-class statistic — and not its order-management API.

Sources: OpenDirect 2.1 · OAAA on OpenDirect 2.1 · AdCP static out-of-home design draft · last verified 2026-09-08

For the digital half of a German estate the lineage is different again: IDOOH, formerly the Digital Media Institute, publishes German DOOH venue-taxonomy and media-data standards that are deliberately distinct from OpenOOH's with a stated mapping intent. A German-to-international venue crosswalk therefore exists for digital screens and has no equivalent for classic panels.

Source: IDOOH standards · last verified 2026-09-08

What the gap implies for a German seller

The analysis this page condenses drew a set of practical consequences. They are listed as consequences of the field-level findings above, not as advice about which protocol to prefer — the ordering follows what unblocks the most downstream work.

  1. The specification is the first dependency. Nothing below is safe while about 24 file types and roughly 220 record bytes are reconstructions, so obtaining the specification and its appendices from the association comes first.
  2. The canonical model cannot be SDAW. Panel identity, geo, fixture class, physical size, cycle calendar and audience value with its methodology belong in one internal model, with SDAW's master-data and availability files as one adapter into it.
  3. The time model has to be resolved once. Decade plan plus pasting block to real date ranges, with concrete start and end dates in every external interface, because no protocol will maintain the calendar.
  4. An AdCP binding is buildable now, against a moving target. Custom print creative formats, installments for cycles, out-of-home metrics with panel identifiers and geotagged evidence, and a seller-modeled estimation basis for the German audience figure — with panel detail in an extension object where no field exists.
  5. The requirements are only representable if they are filed. A panel identifier type, a measurement authority for the German currency, an operator-estimated methodology tier, illumination as a fixture class and posting-cycle fields are all open questions upstream rather than settled omissions.
  6. For AAMP, the available surface is the targeting extension point — binding an out-of-home vocabulary into it the way the United Kingdom's frame register and audience currency were bound into OpenDirect, rather than waiting for an AAMP out-of-home profile.
  7. Digital and classic belong in one product model. German DOOH venue types map to OpenOOH for the digital side, while classic panels carry their own site class until a static taxonomy exists.

What is still unresolved

These are the questions this page's research could not close. They are carried in the project's research notes and are the starting point for the next refresh cycle rather than claims.

  • Whether SDAW is still actively exchanged in German poster trading in 2026, and whether B|A|M still maintains it. No public source discusses the format after roughly 2013.
  • The contents of the members-only specification: about 24 file types, roughly 220 bytes of the master-data record including one 140-byte block, the site-class and price-group appendices, the character set, the filename mask and the transport.
  • Whether the file-type codes for reservation, confirmation, posting order, proof of posting and invoicing exist under names this reconstruction never saw.
  • Whether any SDAW-to-IDOOH or SDAW-to-OpenOOH mapping exists. None was located.
  • Whether AdCP's static out-of-home draft advances into a released schema, and on what version, since the identifiers a German binding would need are drafted rather than shipped.
  • Whether AAMP's out-of-home intent produces an artifact, or whether its OpenDirect-derived vocabulary remains the only out-of-home surface it has.
  • Whether any German or wider DACH media owner has an agentic out-of-home implementation, which the channel deep dive also carries as an open question.
