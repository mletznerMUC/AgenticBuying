SDAW against the agentic protocols

A market deep dive inside the out-of-home channel: SDAW (Standardisierter Datensatz zur Außenwerbung), the German poster industry's exchange format, measured field by field against AAMP (Agentic Advertising Management Protocols) and AdCP (Ad Context Protocol). The finding that organizes everything else is that SDAW's gap is one of transport and digital data model, not of process scope — it describes a complete commercial chain from panel master data to invoicing, in a shape no agent can call.

What this page rests on

The field positions are validated against four production SDAW files from one lessee. This is an association document that is not publicly downloadable, so a reader cannot re-check it the way they can re-check an AdCP schema — which is what the badge on this paragraph, and on the head of every norm-based section below, marks.

Source: SDAW v004, revision date 11 June 2025, B|A|M Bundesverband Aussenmedien (association document, not publicly retrievable; read by the author) · field positions validated against one lessee's production SDAW files · last verified 2026-09-09

Three limits belong with that, and they are limits of a different kind from the ones this page carried before.

  • The production files are from fiscal year 2017. They are one lessee's real master data, availability, performance values and dispatch addresses. The format they demonstrate is current — the norm has been amended as recently as 11 June 2025 — but the contents are eight years old, so no statement about what German poster inventory looks like today is drawn from them.
  • The extraction is partial. Structured extracts of the norm live in this repository, and each one records what has not yet been transcribed. Where a count below is the norm's own and the underlying list is not yet extracted, the text says so rather than implying a completeness the extract does not have.
  • One lessee is not the market. Distributional findings describe that operator's estate. Structural findings — a field's existence, width, value range or absence — are the norm's, and hold market-wide.

reported The three open-source repositories this page's first version was built from — open-sdaw, sdxist and sdaw-import, published between 2011 and 2013 — remain useful as historical evidence that an independent implementation existed and as corroboration of individual field offsets. They are no longer treated as a field authority anywhere on this site. Where a position below still comes from them rather than from the norm, the table says so.

What the master-data record carries

primary-nonpublic The STA data record is a minimum of 499 characters and grows beyond that through two repeating loops. It sits inside a file with a 36-character header record, a summary record of at least 111 characters and a 20-character trailer. Positions are 1-based character offsets.

The block that was read as a gap

This page previously reported an unbroken 140-byte block in the master-data record as undocumented, and roughly 220 of at least 495 bytes as unknown in total. The norm accounts for the block. Position 256 holds Stellennr. bis, two characters, closing a panel-number range that begins at 254. Position 258 begins the FAW description sheet: 138 characters, one criterion per position, X where the criterion applies. Within that block, Z in positions 53 to 55 denotes Zentrum — a central location.

That is a substantial piece of structured site classification that the reconstruction could not see at all: 138 orthogonal attributes per panel, positionally encoded, alongside the site class in Stellenart. Neither AAMP nor AdCP has anywhere to put it other than an extension object.

Source: SDAW v004, revision date 11 June 2025, B|A|M Bundesverband Aussenmedien (association document, not publicly retrievable; read by the author) · field positions validated against one lessee's production SDAW files · last verified 2026-09-09

The two loops

The record's variable length comes from two repeating loops, and both carry commercial meaning rather than padding.

  • Product exclusions, indexed A to Z and capped at 60 characters, holding up to 20 product codes the panel may not carry. See the trading section below.
  • Network list, indexed B to Y, holding the networks a panel belongs to.

Source: SDAW v004, revision date 11 June 2025, B|A|M Bundesverband Aussenmedien (association document, not publicly retrievable; read by the author) · last verified 2026-09-09

Panel identity: SDAW has it, the protocols have nowhere to put it

primary-nonpublic This is the finding that most changes the comparison. SDAW carries the QID, an eight-digit panel identifier issued from the central FAW database. It is market-wide and independent of the lessee: it survives a change of operator, and it is bound to the panel's physical reality — its geographic coordinate, its direction of effect, its height and its carrier form.

Its rules are unusually precise for a fixed-width format:

  • A QID becomes invalid if the panel is moved more than 10 meters, or if its direction changes by more than 45 degrees.
  • It carries no encoded information of its own — it is an opaque key, not a composite code.
  • Every face of a mechanical changer shares one QID.
  • Issued QIDs are retained permanently; they are not recycled.

The earlier version of this page named lessee-scoped site numbering as SDAW's structural weakness and the reason no market-wide panel identity could be handed to an agent. That reading was wrong, and it inverts: SDAW is not missing the identity — the protocols are missing the field to put it in. A German lessee already has exactly the stable, operator- independent, geographically anchored identifier that agentic inventory addressing needs.

Source: SDAW v004, revision date 11 June 2025, B|A|M Bundesverband Aussenmedien (association document, not publicly retrievable; read by the author) · last verified 2026-09-09

speculative The concrete consequence for AdCP, stated as a proposal rather than as a finding: its static out-of-home draft already proposes a panel_id operator unit identifier and {authority}:{id} measurement references. A German binding would need an identifier type of the form faw-qid:<eight digits> — carrying a market-wide identifier rather than an operator-scoped one — and agma: as a measurement authority for Plakatseher pro Stelle. Neither exists today, and neither is proposed by anyone but this page.

The comparison moves in SDAW's favor, and it should be said plainly. The earlier reading held that SDAW's weakest steps were exactly where AdCP's pre-release work is strongest. That was an artifact of the reconstruction: the steps looked weak because they were invisible, not because they were absent. On the norm, SDAW's process coverage is the more complete of the two — it reaches settlement, which neither agentic protocol does, and it has carried complaint handling and posting evidence as first-class record types for years. What SDAW lacks is a way to call any of it.

Posting dates are a rule, not a lookup

primary-nonpublic This page previously described SDAW's time model as an index resolvable only through an annually re-issued plan carried as data. The published calendar is generated from rules, and the rules are stated. For 2027 the calendar defines 34 decades and 52 calendar weeks, each with three pasting blocks and a set of derived deadlines:

Quantity Rule
Pasting block B Block A start plus 3 days
Pasting block C Block A start plus 7 days
Double decades Decades 01 and 34 run 14 days; all others are standard length
Cancellation deadline Start minus 60 days
Poster arrival Five working days before the day preceding the start
Motif instruction Start minus 28 days for decades, minus 21 days for weeks
Weekly posting Always begins on a Tuesday

Source: B|A|M Bundesverband Aussenmedien, 2027 poster posting-date calendar, sheet BASIS (association document, not publicly retrievable; read by the author) · dekadenplan-2027.json · last verified 2026-09-09

An agent can therefore compute posting dates rather than look them up — but only as a default. B|A|M attaches three caveats to the calendar it publishes: no warranty is given, actual dates may differ by plus or minus one day, and individual municipalities may operate special arrangements. The practical rule that follows is narrow and worth stating exactly: an agent may quote these dates as a working assumption and must not present them as a commitment. A concrete date is confirmed by the lessee, not derived.

This still does not close the protocol-side gap. AdCP's static out-of-home draft states that the protocol will not maintain a posting calendar, so the decade grid stays in the seller's systems either way. What changes is the cost of the adapter: deriving dates from seven rules is a smaller and more testable problem than carrying a re-issued table every year.

Source: AdCP static out-of-home design draft · last verified 2026-09-09

Product exclusions as a trading dimension

primary-nonpublic Each panel can carry up to 20 product exclusions, drawn from an appendix listing 236 product codes in 25 main groups. The exclusion sits on the inventory: it says what this specific panel may not carry, independently of any buyer. The list was extended on 26 November 2024 with political categories in the range 260 to 269 and religious categories in the range 270 to 273, alongside product exclusions for election advertising.

Source: SDAW v004, revision date 11 June 2025, appendix B, B|A|M Bundesverband Aussenmedien (association document, not publicly retrievable; read by the author) · produktliste.json · last verified 2026-09-09

Neither AAMP nor AdCP models inventory-side category exclusion at this granularity. Both treat brand safety and category avoidance as buyer-side constructs — something a buyer declares about a campaign, matched against content or context signals. A panel outside a school that may not carry alcohol, or a site in front of a place of worship that may not carry a competing denomination, is a property of the physical location and its permit, and it survives every change of buyer. On both protocols today it would have to travel in an extension object, and a buyer-side avoidance list cannot substitute for it: the two express different facts, and only one of them is enforceable by the seller.

Sources: AdCP static out-of-home design draft · IAB Tech Lab Agentic Deals API · last verified 2026-09-09

Network trading

primary-nonpublic SDAW gained a network-trading layer on 25 June 2019: SNZ for network master data, AUN, OPN and STN for booking, option and cancellation, and BSN and SBN for confirmation. A panel's membership travels in the network list loop of its master-data record, and the sentinel value NetzKzf-1 = 999999 marks a panel as bookable only within a network, never individually.

Source: SDAW v004, revision date 11 June 2025, B|A|M Bundesverband Aussenmedien (association document, not publicly retrievable; read by the author) · last verified 2026-09-09

This is the closest thing SDAW has to an AdCP product or an AAMP package, and the earlier version of this page did not mention it at all — a consequence of reading the format through a toolchain that predates the layer by six years. It matters for a mapping in a specific way: a network is not a convenience bundle but a unit of sale with its own booking, option and cancellation records, and a panel flagged 999999 cannot be represented as an individually addressable item without misstating what the seller will accept.

The limits of the coordinate format

primary-nonpublic Coordinates have been WGS84 since 7 May 2003, in the fixed format 99.999999999999 at positions 466 and 481, with the earlier MIA pair at 222 and 237 retained alongside rather than replaced. The format is two integer digits and twelve decimals.

Source: SDAW v004, revision date 11 June 2025, B|A|M Bundesverband Aussenmedien (association document, not publicly retrievable; read by the author) · field positions validated against one lessee's production SDAW files · last verified 2026-09-09

Twelve decimal places is sub-millimeter resolution — far beyond anything a survey of a poster site produces, and beyond what the QID's own tolerance requires, given that a panel may move up to 10 meters before its identifier is invalidated. The precision is spurious, and it is the harmless half of the problem.

The consequential half is what the format cannot express. Two integer digits and no sign position mean no negative longitudes and nothing at or above 100 degrees. The field is implicitly restricted to central Europe, and the sign convention is never stated, because within Germany it never has to be. Any consumer that treats these as general decimal degrees inherits an unstated assumption; any exporter that meets a site west of the prime meridian has no representation for it.

DOOH is an object class here, not a delivery model

primary-nonpublic SDAW does represent digital sites — as object classes. The digital main site classes are VD, GD, SD, RD, and, added 11 June 2025, KD (Kleinfläche digital) and FD (Flag digital); SD and ED also exist as digital sub classes under GF, added 26 November 2024. A digital panel is, structurally, a panel with a different class code.

The single statement about digital delivery anywhere in the document is a clause in that 26 November 2024 changelog entry: the digital GF sub classes run on a decade rhythm, with a committed 10-second spot per minute.

Source: SDAW v004, revision date 11 June 2025, appendices A and E, B|A|M Bundesverband Aussenmedien (association document, not publicly retrievable; read by the author) · objektarten.json · last verified 2026-09-09

The negative finding is stronger evidence than the positive one, because it can be stated exactly. Across the norm's 4,278 lines there is not one occurrence of any of: DOOH, programmatic, API, Schnittstelle, XML, JSON, REST, SSP, DSP, RTB, impression, playout, CMS or web service. There is no field for spot length, share of voice, loop length, plays per period, creative format, resolution or proof of play.

So the digital estate is bookable through SDAW only as though it were paper: a class of site, sold by period, on a decade rhythm. Every concept a digital out-of-home agent would negotiate over is outside the format. This is the sharpest form of the page's overall finding — the norm's process model is complete and its digital data model does not exist.

Source: SDAW v004, revision date 11 June 2025, B|A|M Bundesverband Aussenmedien (association document, not publicly retrievable; read by the author) · last verified 2026-09-09

Encoding, transport, and the rules that settle conflicts

primary-nonpublic Four things this page previously listed as unknown or inferred are stated by the norm.

Source: SDAW v004, revision date 11 June 2025, appendices C and D, B|A|M Bundesverband Aussenmedien (association document, not publicly retrievable; read by the author) · last verified 2026-09-09

Two of these change how a mapping should be built. The price precedence chain means a price is not a single value on an inventory item but the last value in an ordered sequence of records — an adapter that reads STA alone will quote stale prices. The performance-value rule is stronger than anything either agentic protocol requires: the measurement basis is constrained to two named industry sources, a third value is admissible only as the outcome of a formal complaint, and a seller's own estimate is prohibited outright. The value is a week or decade figure; a daily figure is derived by dividing by 7 or by 10.5.

That last point sharpens the audience-currency row below. AdCP's out-of-home metrics require an estimation basis to be declared and treat a seller-modeled estimate as an acceptable tier. SDAW does not permit that tier at all. A German lessee moving onto AdCP would be declaring, as a seller estimate, a number its own market rules forbid it from estimating.

The gap in both directions

What SDAW would need to be agentically transactable

The list is shorter than it was, and every item on it is now about transport or digital delivery rather than about commercial scope.

  • A request and response surface. The unit of exchange is a ZIP archive on a filename mask. There is no query, no task lifecycle and no synchronous answer.
  • Credentials, signatures and idempotency. Registration at the FAW governs who may exchange files; nothing in the format authenticates a message or makes an operation safely repeatable.
  • Digital playout concepts. No screen, loop, slot, spot length, share of voice or proof of play — the whole vocabulary is absent, as the line-level check above shows.
  • An impression currency. One performance value per panel and period, billed in days rather than in contacts.
  • Robustness against positional drift. A single wrong character shifts every following field, and the format has no self-description.

Source: SDAW v004, revision date 11 June 2025, B|A|M Bundesverband Aussenmedien (association document, not publicly retrievable; read by the author) · last verified 2026-09-09

What the protocols would need to represent German classic out-of-home

This list has grown, because the norm shows more that the protocols have nowhere to put.

  • A bookable panel object, and an identifier type able to carry a market-wide QID rather than an operator-scoped face reference.
  • A static out-of-home property type in AdCP, without which static inventory has no seller-authorization path of the kind other channels use.
  • Geo on inventory. Coordinates are targeting input and evidence metadata on both sides.
  • Inventory-side category exclusions, which neither protocol models at all.
  • A posting-cycle grid, or at least a place to declare the derivation rules and their tolerance.
  • Illumination and physical dimensions on inventory, rather than illuminated hours in a report and print specifications on a creative.
  • A measurement authority for markets whose currency is neither seller-estimated nor one of the named national bodies.
  • Cost per frame in AAMP, which its own OpenDirect base has and its pricing enumeration does not.

Sources: AdCP static out-of-home design draft · IAB Tech Lab Agentic Deals API · OpenDirect 2.1 · last verified 2026-09-09

Where the nearest out-of-home data model actually sits

The closest thing to an IAB out-of-home data model is not AAMP but OpenDirect 2.1, built in 2024 with the out-of-home trade bodies: it has frames, named audience datasources, share of time, cost per frame and a proof-of-posting statistics object. AAMP inherits that vocabulary shell through its OpenDirect-based order surface without binding out-of-home semantics to it. AdCP's static out-of-home draft reaches the same conclusion from the other side, stating that it borrows OpenDirect's vocabulary — lead times, flight bounds, frame references and proof of posting as a first-class statistic — and not its order-management API.

For the digital half of a German estate the lineage is different again: IDOOH, formerly the Digital Media Institute, publishes German DOOH venue-taxonomy and media-data standards that are deliberately distinct from OpenOOH's with a stated mapping intent. A German-to-international venue crosswalk therefore exists for digital screens and has no equivalent for classic panels — and, as the section above shows, SDAW's own digital coverage is a set of class codes rather than a screen model, so the two do not meet.

What this implies for a German seller

These follow from the field-level findings above. They are consequences, not advice about which protocol to prefer, and the ordering follows what unblocks the most downstream work.

  1. The canonical model can be closer to SDAW than previously assumed. QID, site class with its 138 criteria, coordinates, illumination, physical size, the decade grid and product exclusions are all already modeled, and modeled market-wide. What an internal model has to add is a digital screen model and a transport, not a panel model.
  2. QID is the identifier to expose. It is stable across operator changes, geographically anchored and market-wide — the properties an agentic identifier needs. Its invalidation rules, 10 meters and 45 degrees, are also the rules an inventory sync has to implement.
  3. The time model is a derivation, not a table. Seven rules generate the decade and week calendar. Implement them once, carry the plus-or-minus-one-day tolerance explicitly, and publish concrete dates in every external interface, because no protocol will maintain the calendar.
  4. An AdCP binding is buildable now, against a moving target. Custom print creative formats, installments for cycles, out-of-home metrics with panel identifiers and geotagged evidence — with QID, the criteria block and product exclusions in an extension object where no field exists.
  5. The audience value needs an authority, not an estimate. SDAW forbids seller-set values outright, so declaring Plakatseher pro Stelle as a seller-modeled estimate misrepresents its status. This is a requirement to file upstream rather than a mapping to choose.
  6. Product exclusions have no home and need one. Filing them as an inventory-side concept is the only route by which a seller's legal and permit obligations survive into an agentic transaction.
  7. For AAMP, the available surface is the targeting extension point — binding an out-of-home vocabulary into it the way the United Kingdom's frame register and audience currency were bound into OpenDirect.

What is still unresolved

These are the questions this page's research could not close. They are carried in the project's research notes and are the starting point for the next refresh cycle rather than claims. Several of the older ones are closed by the norm and no longer appear.

  • The remaining eight of the norm's 29 record types, and the full field layouts of STA, FRE, LWT and VSA. This is a transcription backlog in this site's extracts, not an unknown.
  • Which of the norm's 4,278 lines the LWT record-length contradiction sits in, and whether B|A|M treats 63 or 64 as correct.
  • Whether the three appendix A anomalies — SES, the doubled SD, and VD CF alongside FD — are errata or deliberate.
  • How widely SDAW is exchanged today, and by which lessees. The norm's maintenance through June 2025 establishes that it is maintained; it does not establish volume.
  • speculative Whether a modernization toward an API-based successor architecture is under discussion in the market. No timetable has been published, and this page carries no detail beyond that.
  • Whether any SDAW-to-IDOOH or SDAW-to-OpenOOH mapping exists. None was located.
  • Whether AdCP's static out-of-home draft advances into a released schema, and on what version, since the identifiers a German binding would need are drafted rather than shipped.
  • Whether AAMP's out-of-home intent produces an artifact, or whether its OpenDirect-derived vocabulary remains the only out-of-home surface it has.
  • Whether any German or wider DACH media owner has an agentic out-of-home implementation, which the channel deep dive also carries as an open question.
