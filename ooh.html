Out-of-home as an agentic channel

A channel deep dive on OOH (out-of-home) and DOOH (digital out-of-home): which parts of the agentic buying protocols reach the medium, what has actually been piloted and where, and why a medium that addresses places rather than people fits the protocols' primitives badly.

Updated 4 September 2026 · all claims on this page verified on that date

Claims are badged by evidence strength; unbadged statements are verified against their cited source.

  • shipped released or in production
  • announced committed, not yet shipped
  • reported secondary sources only, or disputed
  • speculative interpretation, not settled fact

Two layers, moving at different speeds

Out-of-home has two distinct layers of machine-readable support, and conflating them is the most common error in coverage of the channel. The programmatic rails are mature: OpenRTB 2.6 added a DOOH object and an impression-multiplier object in April 2022, AdCOM defines a matching DOOH object, OpenDirect 2.1 added guaranteed direct trading for out-of-home in October 2024, and the OpenOOH venue taxonomy has given the industry a shared venue vocabulary for years. None of that is agentic; all of it predates the agentic protocols, and a demand-side platform has been able to bid on a physical screen and estimate an audience per play for some time.

Sources: OpenRTB 2.6 specification; AdCOM 1.0; OAAA on OpenDirect · last verified 2026-09-04

The agentic layer on top is thin and new. AdCP's (Ad Context Protocol) structured out-of-home schema work sits in an unreleased pre-release line; AAMP (Agentic Advertising Management Protocols) names out-of-home as future scope rather than current coverage. The consequence for a buyer is specific: the channel's programmatic plumbing is older than its agentic vocabulary, and the two do not yet meet in a stable release.

What the protocols cover today

AdCP

DOOH was one of the twenty media channels AdCP recognized at its 3.0 general availability — the first stable release to name the medium at all — alongside broadcast television and audio. reported The channel documentation goes further than a label: AdCP's DOOH creative channel defines proof-of-play impression-tracker macros, including a screen identifier, a play timestamp, venue latitude and longitude, and a venue type, fired when a creative displays on a physical screen. Its flat-rate product schema types DOOH deals directly, with fields for share of voice, loop duration, minimum plays per hour, venue package, duration, and daypart, and geo targeting expressed as travel-time isochrones, a radius, or GeoJSON. Both of those descriptions rest on secondary technical synthesis and on search-indexed extracts of the documentation rather than on pages opened directly, so they are marked as resting on sources this site could not read at first hand.

Sources: AdCP DOOH creative channel documentation; AdCP — what's new in v3; No Fluff Advisory, DOOH and place-based media standards · last verified 2026-09-04

announced The richer, structured out-of-home work is confined to the unreleased 3.2 pre-release line, which reached v3.2.0-rc.0 on September 3, 2026 with no stable 3.2 release. That line adds DOOH placement attributes for slot duration, loop duration, screen resolution, and motion; an out-of-home metrics block for static and classic inventory covering panels, posting periods, share of voice, illuminated hours, and a modeled impression estimate; a DOOH sales specialism; share of voice defined as a duration-weighted time share; and versioned OpenOOH venue identifiers. The same line carries connected-television experience profiles and audio delivery, so out-of-home arrives as one of several channel extensions rather than as a standalone module.

Sources: AdCP CHANGELOG.md; AdCP releases · last verified 2026-09-04

One entry in that line is worth quoting for what it concedes. The static-out-of-home contract states that static units have no play events, that delivery is a period-level modeled audience estimate, and that settlement rests on proof of posting per OAAA conventions. A protocol built for counted impressions is, in that sentence, writing down the fact that a poster cannot be counted.

Source: AdCP CHANGELOG.md, 3.2.0-beta.7 entry · last verified 2026-09-04

AAMP

reported IAB Tech Lab's AAMP standards page describes the initiative as applying globally to digital advertising, with out-of-home — alongside linear television and radio — named as a planned future extension rather than as current scope. An independent comparison analysis reaches the same conclusion from the code: DOOH appears in AAMP's OpenDirect-based agent software development kits only as media-type enumeration values and demonstration catalog entries, with no dedicated transaction path, in explicit contrast to the dedicated path linear television receives.

Sources: IAB Tech Lab — AAMP; No Fluff Advisory, "AdCP vs AAMP" · last verified 2026-09-04

The component repositories bear that out. IAB Tech Lab's Agentic Deals API carries a dooh value in its inventory-type enumeration, alongside site, app, audio, endemic, and other — a classification with no venue, screen, or loop schema underneath it. The agentic-direct reference implementation, an A2A and MCP wrapper around OpenDirect 2.1, references a DOOH extension in its OpenAPI metadata but exposes no DOOH-specific tools, tasks, or fields beyond that pointer, and its geo targeting reaches no further than the coordinates already in AdCOM. reported The gap looks like neglect rather than intent: the wrapper's most recent commit predates AAMP 2.3 by roughly six months, while the OpenDirect base underneath it has carried out-of-home support since October 2024.

Sources: IABTechLab/deal-api; IABTechLab/agentic-direct; agentic-direct commit history · last verified 2026-09-04

The comparison page covers the two protocols' general divergence; out-of-home is one of the sharper instances of it, and the direction is the reverse of the usual framing. AAMP is the protocol family built by extending existing IAB Tech Lab standards — including the very standards that already support out-of-home — and it is the one with no out-of-home path.

The standards the channel already runs on

An agentic out-of-home buy inherits a stack of pre-agentic standards. They are what an agent is ultimately negotiating over, and their governance sits with the out-of-home trade bodies — OAAA in the United States, Outsmart in the United Kingdom, the Outdoor Media Association in Australia — as much as with IAB Tech Lab.

Medium-specific problems and the solutions proposed

Every problem below follows from one fact: out-of-home addresses a place, and the agentic protocols were designed to address a person. Where a solution exists it is usually in the programmatic rails, not in the agentic layer; where the agentic layer has an answer, it is usually in AdCP's unreleased 3.2 line.

Problem What breaks Where the answer stands Status
No identifier, no click An impression is a multiplier applied to a play, not a per-user event OpenRTB's Qty object carries a fractional, publisher-disclosed multiplier per play shipped
Identity-based primitives Audiences, frequency caps and reach all assume a resolvable person or device AdCP's 3.2 line adds identity-absence and reach-methodology declarations, and requires a reach unit on frequency-only delivery announced
Static OOH has no play event A poster is up or it is not; there is nothing to count and no bid to make AdCP's 3.2 line gives static inventory its own metrics block settling on proof of posting, per OAAA conventions, rather than forcing it through the digital schema announced
Proof of play Delivery proof rests on player logs and sensors rather than on an ad server's own count AdCP's DOOH creative channel documents proof-of-play macros: screen identifier, play timestamp, venue coordinates, venue type reported
Screen, venue or network A buyer's intent can sit at any of three granularities, and the agent has to know which The OpenOOH taxonomy supplies the venue vocabulary; AdCP's 3.2 placements reference versioned OpenOOH identifiers announced
Loop share and slot scheduling A buyer's share is a fraction of loop time, not a guaranteed exclusive slot AdCP's 3.2 line defines share of voice as a duration-weighted time share and adds slot contiguity parameters and slot-to-loop validation announced
Creative heterogeneity Resolution, orientation and motion capability vary from screen to screen inside one network AdCP's 3.2 placement attributes carry screen resolution and motion per placement announced
Playout latency Bids resolve in roughly 100 to 300 milliseconds, yet some inventory is prefetched minutes or hours before it plays No agentic-layer answer located; agent negotiation sits at planning time, outside the bid path reported
Measurement fragmentation Geopath, Route and national bodies use different methods and are not interoperable currencies The Geopath, OAAA and Ipsos pilot targets a next-generation United States currency; no cross-border harmonization initiative was located announced
Public-space compliance Local content rules and venue restrictions attach to physical space, not to an ad slot In the one documented end-to-end agentic campaign, compliance stayed in the human oversight layer rather than being delegated to the agents reported

Sources: OpenRTB 2.6 · AdCP CHANGELOG.md · AdCP DOOH creative channel · OAAA on OpenOOH v1.2.1 · Trillboards on the DOOH bid stream · Geopath, OAAA and Ipsos · invidis on the Broadsign campaign · last verified 2026-09-04

Two rows deserve emphasis because they are where the medium and the protocols meet most awkwardly. announced The identity row is the one AdCP addresses most directly: its 3.2 line adds product-scoped identity-absence declarations and DOOH sales conformance for rejecting a frequency cap outright, which is a protocol conceding that a request it is designed to accept cannot be honored in this medium. The latency row is the one nobody addresses: real-time bidding on playout is a legacy engineering constraint that agent negotiation works around by sitting at planning time, and no primary statement from either protocol was located that engages with it.

Sources: AdCP CHANGELOG.md, 3.2.0-rc.0 entry; Trillboards · last verified 2026-09-04

The one end-to-end case, and what it does not prove

reported The best-documented agent-to-agent out-of-home buy is a campaign for the Dutch charity lottery Lot of Happiness, announced from Montreal on May 27, 2026: Broadsign's sell-side agent and Draft Digital's buy-side agent communicated over AdCP across Global Netherlands inventory, covering audience and venue targeting, media selection, campaign setup, creative workflow, approvals and execution, and delivered more than 830,000 impressions across supermarkets, malls, gas stations and city screens. Every account also records that human oversight and guardrails were retained throughout — including for compliance with local regulations and restrictions. It is badged as resting on secondary sources because that is exactly what it rests on: the campaign is corroborated across seven or more independent trade outlets, but none of the primary releases could be opened directly from this project's research environment, and the "first-ever fully agentic" framing is the participants' own and is not independently verifiable.

Sources: OAAA; Digital Signage Today; invidis · last verified 2026-09-04

reported The geographic pattern is worth stating plainly: every genuine agentic out-of-home case located is European or North American. The regions where nothing was found are not quiet — JCDecaux's Latin American programmatic connectivity and the Gulf's mall and airport contracts are substantial developments — but they are programmatic supply and direct sales, not agent-mediated buying. Given this project's own vantage point in the German market, the DACH gap was searched deliberately and separately; the only 2026 Ströer AI advertising initiative located is a conversational in-ad creative format from its Seeding Alliance subsidiary, which is neither agentic buying nor out-of-home.

Sources: PPC Land; meedia.de on the Ströer format · last verified 2026-09-04

Cross-channel: holding out-of-home in the same plan

A channel deep dive that stops at the channel boundary misses the point of agentic buying, whose premise is one brief resolving across every screen. Two things can be said about out-of-home in that picture, and both are about absence rather than achievement.

AdCP's design intent points toward a shared schema rather than a set of channel silos: the same stretch of the 3.2 pre-release line that added out-of-home placement attributes and static posting evidence also added connected-television experience profiles against the IAB CTV Ad Portfolio and audio delivery with demographic notation. speculative Reading that as a deliberate cross-channel product abstraction is an interpretation, not a stated goal — no primary statement to that effect was located — and in any case nothing in the line has shipped, so no buyer can build a cross-channel plan against it yet.

Source: AdCP CHANGELOG.md · last verified 2026-09-04

reported The gaps are more concrete than the intent. No source was located describing a working unified reach-and-frequency methodology that includes out-of-home alongside connected television and mobile, agent-mediated or otherwise; 2026 commentary on cross-channel reach concentrates on linear television, connected television and digital. And no source was located describing an agent reallocating budget between out-of-home and another channel mid-flight. Every agentic out-of-home case above is single-channel: a plan or a buy inside out-of-home, not a plan that trades it off against anything else. The workflows page documents the cross-platform agentic buys that do exist, across linear and digital video — out-of-home is not among them.

Source: survey of 2026 cross-channel reach and frequency coverage and of every case listed above; no qualifying source located · last verified 2026-09-04

The structural reason sits in Figure 1. A cross-channel frequency cap needs a common unit across channels, and out-of-home's unit is a modeled audience per play attached to a place. Until an agent can convert between "reached this person three times" and "was present at this junction during these dayparts," out-of-home enters a cross-channel plan as a budget line rather than as a comparable reach contribution.

What is still unresolved

These are the open questions this page's research could not close; they are the starting point for the next refresh cycle rather than claims.

  • Whether VIOOH's Q3 2026 self-serve rollout and its stated AdCP and AAMP registrations have actually happened. Both were described as planned in June 2026, the quarter is nearly over, and no completion announcement was found.
  • Whether any DACH-region agentic out-of-home pilot exists that a broader German-language search would surface. The absence recorded above rests on a targeted search, not an exhaustive one.
  • Whether AdCP's DOOH creative-channel documentation and flat-rate DOOH schema read as described. Both sit behind an egress block for direct fetch from this project's research environment, so the claims above are triangulated from search-indexed extracts and are deliberately badged more conservatively than the underlying facts probably warrant.
  • Whether AAMP's Agentic Ad Object, described as derived from AdCOM, preserves AdCOM's native DOOH object. Its schema was not located as an independently fetchable file.
  • Whether Perion's and Magnite's agent products touch out-of-home inventory specifically, or are cross-channel products reported alongside out-of-home growth figures.
  • The publication date of the IAB and MRC DOOH Measurement Guide, which no located source dates reliably.
